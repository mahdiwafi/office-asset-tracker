# Taking care of equipment

Borrowed equipment is shared property, and everyone's work depends on it staying in good shape. This page covers the basics of keeping it that way.

## Everyday handling

- Carry laptops and tablets in their sleeve or bag when moving around — a loose laptop in a backpack with keys and a water bottle is the most common way screens crack.
- Keep food and drink away from keyboards and laptops. If you do spill, power off, disconnect the charger, and bring it to ICT — most spills are fixable if reported fast.
- Do not peel off stickers, key caps, or screen protectors; they are part of the checked-out condition.
- Report small problems (a wobbling hinge, a dead battery cell) as soon as you notice them. Small problems repaired early stay free; small problems ignored become big problems you may be charged for.

## Charging

Use the charger that came with the item. A wrong charger can damage the battery or, worse, the charging port — which is the most expensive single repair we do. If your charger stops working, bring it in for a swap; do not borrow a colleague's.

## Updates and security

- Keep the device updated: install updates when the office asks, and do not postpone them more than a few days. Updates close security holes that affect everyone on the office network, not just your device.
- Do not disable security software, the firewall, or the automatic lock. If the security software causes a problem, tell ICT — we will fix the setting properly rather than leave the device unprotected.
- Lock your screen whenever you step away. A lost, unlocked laptop is how offices get breached.

## Travel

You may take borrowed equipment on business travel, including abroad — no special permission is needed for the standard items. Take the charger and the bag. If the item is stolen or damaged while travelling, the same 24-hour reporting rule applies (see Damage and loss).

## Data

Your files live on the device and in the office cloud. Cloud copies protect you from hardware failure — if you only store files locally and the device fails, the files are gone with it. When in doubt, save to the office drive. Back up anything you cannot replace.

## Personal use

Moderate personal use (watching video, personal email) is fine. Do not install pirated software, torrent clients, or unknown browser extensions, and do not lend your borrowed device to family or friends — the device is issued to you, and anything done on it is traced to you.

## What voids coverage

Repairs, parts and damage are covered by the office (see Damage and loss) unless the item was misused: opened or disassembled, repaired outside ICT, used with non-approved parts, or damaged through deliberate misuse. When in doubt, ask before you do it — a two-minute question is cheaper than a written-off laptop.
