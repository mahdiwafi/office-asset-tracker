# Who can borrow what, and how priority works

Most equipment can be borrowed by anyone who works here. This page explains the few exceptions and how we decide who gets an item when several people want it at once.

## What everyone can borrow

Standard items — laptops, monitors, headsets, keyboards, mice, dongles, cables — are available to all staff, volunteers and long-term contractors, regardless of role or seniority. There is no queue for seniority and no first-come-first-served advantage for most equipment: approvals are based on the reason for the request, not on how early you asked.

## High-demand items

A short list of items is marked "high demand" in the tracker: high-spec laptops, phones, cameras, and projectors. When several requests compete for the same item, priority goes to:

1. operational need — the person who needs it to do a core job task that day
2. external commitments — a client meeting, a workshop, a filmed event
3. accessibility and medical need — a larger screen, a specific keyboard layout, a quieter mouse
4. request order, as the tie-breaker

The priority is decided by the approver, who can see the request notes and the calendar. If your request is refused because someone else had priority, you stay on the list and the item becomes available to you when their need ends.

## Waitlists

Refused or delayed requests go onto a waitlist automatically. When the item comes back, the first person on the list is offered it before it is offered as walk-in stock. You can watch your position in the tracker; if you no longer need the item, remove yourself from the list so the next person is not kept waiting.

## Cohorts and projects

At the start of internships, cohorts or large projects, ICT may reserve a pool of equipment (for example twenty laptops for a training week). Reserved equipment is shown as "reserved" in the tracker and cannot be borrowed until the event ends. Plan your requests around these dates.

## Equipment for accessibility

If you need equipment because of a medical condition or accessibility requirement — a screen reader, an ergonomic chair, a special keyboard — you do not compete with other borrowers. Raise the request with "accessibility" in the notes; it is approved without priority ranking and your manager cannot refuse it without discussing an alternative with you and ICT first.

## Who decides

Day-to-day approvals are made by your manager (for the need) and ICT (for the equipment). If you disagree with a decision, you can ask the office manager to review it — the tracker keeps the full history of the request, so the review is quick.
